Mercedes' return to a near-zero sidepod concept for 2026 is not a retreat to old ideas — it's a rebuild on top of three seasons of cooling and vortex-management data the team collected while the original "zero-pod" experiment was quietly shelved.

## What actually changed

The new bodywork reduces the frontal cooling inlet by roughly 12%, according to onboard thermal-camera footage from pre-season testing, while pushing the undercut deeper into the floor's leading edge. That combination does two things at once: it lowers frontal drag on Monza-style low-downforce configurations, and it feeds a cleaner airflow structure to the floor's forward vortex generators.

## Why it matters for tyre life

Cooler-running sidepods sound like a pure power-unit story, but the second-order effect is on tyre thermal windows. A tighter cooling package changes how much heat the floor rejects back toward the rear tyres in high-speed corners — early sim data suggests a measurable reduction in rear-left thermal degradation on circuits with sustained high-speed direction changes.
