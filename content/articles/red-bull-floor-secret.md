Every team runs floor updates through a season, but Red Bull's version at the last flyaway round drew more attention than most — not for what was visible in the FIA's published technical drawings, but for what onboard footage suggested was happening underneath.

## The tell

High-speed onboard clips through the middle sector show noticeably less porpoising-adjacent ride-height oscillation than the sister car was running two rounds earlier. That's consistent with a floor-edge geometry revision that seals the underfloor low-pressure zone more consistently as ride height changes through a corner — exactly the kind of change a team would rather not draw attention to mid-championship.

## What we're watching next

If the change holds up across a wider range of track types, expect competitors to start referencing it directly in their own upgrade justifications within a few rounds — floor-edge philosophy tends to converge fast once one team demonstrates a clear time gain.
