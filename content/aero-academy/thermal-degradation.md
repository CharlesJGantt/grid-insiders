Heat management is one of the least visible battles in Formula 1, and one of the most decisive over a race distance. Two systems dominate the conversation: brakes and tyres.

## The operating windows

- **Critical:** above 120°C surface temperature, carbon-carbon brake discs begin to lose consistent friction coefficient.
- **Optimal:** 90–110°C is the sweet spot most current tyre compounds are designed around for peak grip without accelerated wear.
- **Cold:** below 80°C core temperature, tyres struggle to generate peak grip at all — a common problem on out-laps and in changeable weather.

## Why it drives strategy

Teams that can keep components inside their optimal windows for longer effectively get more usable pace out of the same physical parts — which is why brake duct design and tyre-blanket practice remain such closely guarded details, even in an era of extensive TV coverage.
